I am an applied economist working on Environmental Economics. My research asks whether public investments in climate adaptation reduce the economic damage of extreme events, using spatial data, newly assembled administrative datasets and causal inference methods.

I am a Ph.D. candidate at the University of Bologna, where I am supervised by Lorenzo Forni and Vincenzo Scrutinio. I am also an Economist at the Prometeia Think Tank. In 2026 I was a visiting researcher at Caltech, hosted by Hannah Druckenmiller, and I will join Caltech as a Postdoctoral Research Associate in January 2027.

Research interests: Environmental Economics, Applied Microeconomics

Job Market Paper

Firms Under Water: Floods, Adaptation and Performance
Best Young Economist Award, Italian Society of Economics (2026)

I estimate the causal effect of ex-ante adaptation on firm revenues, linking public investment records (2003–2024) to river geometries, geocoded firms and flood-extent maps for the May 2023 flood in Emilia-Romagna. Completed preventive works attenuate revenue losses from extreme rainfall; works built to repair past damage do not, suggesting prevention and repair are not substitutes.
